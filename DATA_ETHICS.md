# Data Ethics & Community Statement: `deori`

## 1. Respect for Native Speakers and Community Authority
The `deori` library is a computational and linguistic tool designed to support learners, researchers, and NLP developers. **It is not, and can never be, a substitute for native speakers, elders, and traditional custodians of the Deori language (Deori Cchu).**

The language belongs first and foremost to the Deori community. Any authoritative decisions regarding spelling standards, revitalisation priorities, dialect preservation, and cultural representation must be guided by community speakers and representative bodies, notably the **Deori Sahitya Sabha (দেউৰী সাহিত্য সভা)**.

## 2. Status of the Language
Deori (ISO 639-3: `der`, Glottolog: `deor1238`) is an endangered Tibeto-Burman language of the Bodo-Garo (Sal) branch, spoken in Upper Assam (notably in Lakhimpur, Dhemaji, Tinsukia, and Sivasagar districts) and adjacent districts of Arunachal Pradesh. With a widespread language shift toward Assamese in recent generations, linguistic vitality is fragile.

In this context, computational tools must prioritize accuracy and avoid harmful distortions:
- Preserving dialectal integrity across the traditional divisions: **Dibongiya (দিবঙীয়া)**, **Tengapaniya (তেঙাপনীয়া)**, **Borgonya (বৰগঞা)**, and **Patorgonya (পাতৰগঞা)**.
- Documenting surviving varieties respectfully without imposing an artificial uniformity that obscures living heritage.

## 3. Strict Anti-Hallucination & Provenance Policy
Because endangered languages are uniquely vulnerable to digital contamination:
1. **Zero Hallucination**: No word, morpheme, grammatical rule, or example sentence may ever be invented or generated by speculative AI models.
2. **Mandatory Sourcing**: Every lexical entry, morphological rule, and phonological assertion in this library must carry verifiable bibliographic or archival provenance (`source`, `page`, `year`, `variety`). Unverified forms must remain marked as `TODO: needs source` and omitted from authoritative lexicons.
3. **Dialect Transparency**: Every piece of data must specify its dialect or variety where known. Dibongiya forms must not be presented as pan-Deori when differences exist.
4. **Recording Conflicting Accounts**: When historical sources (e.g. W. B. Brown 1895) and modern community materials or academic grammars (e.g. L. Jacquesson 2005, Nripen Deori, Deori Sahitya Sabha) differ in phonology, tone, or morphology, both claims must be recorded alongside their sources rather than artificially harmonized.

## 4. Copyright, Licensing, and Attribution
- **Public Domain Materials**: Historical works in the public domain (e.g. W. B. Brown, *An Outline Grammar of the Deori Chutiya Language*, 1895) are bundled with appropriate citation.
- **Modern Copyrighted Works**: Modern published grammars, dictionaries, and literary collections (e.g. works by Jacquesson, Dr. Upendranath Goswami, Dr. Sangita Saikia, Saranan Deori, Nripen Deori, Lilakanta Deori, Radhakanta Deori, Deori Sahitya Sabha) may not be copied wholesale into repository distributions without explicit permission. The library instead provides reproducible importer scripts allowing users to load and parse legitimately acquired texts on their own machines.
- **Open Data & Community Commons**: Where community data is licensed under Creative Commons (e.g. CC BY-SA 4.0), proper attribution to original authors, speakers, and recorders must accompany all exports and derivative forms.

## 5. Contact & Community Oversight
We actively welcome corrections, reviews, and guidance from members of the Deori community, educators, and language activists. If any data in this library is inaccurate, culturally sensitive, or used inappropriately, please open an issue or reach out to the maintainers for immediate remediation.
